03 - Which Activity Uses the Most Land?

THE SCALE OF GRAZING

8.8×

Grazing occupies approximately 8.8 times as much agricultural-use land as cropping.

Grazing covers 376.0 million hectares, compared with 42.5 million hectares for cropping—a difference of 333.5 million hectares. Horticulture and intensive agriculture together occupy less than 1 million hectares.

04 - How Has Agricultural Land Changed?

A DECADE OF CHANGE

+32.4% Cropping +10.39 M ha

Australia's agricultural footprint changed unevenly from 2010–11 to 2020–21. Cropping gained about 10.4 million hectares, while grazing fell by 9.5 million hectares. Horticulture and intensive agriculture also grew from much smaller bases.

Grazing accounts for the majority of agricultural land in every state and territory, but Victoria has the most even split between grazing and cropping, unlike most other states where grazing clearly dominates. Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory remain strongly grazing-oriented, creating a clear contrast with Victoria.

09 - Where Are Australia's Broadacre Crops Grown?

CROP PATTERNS ACROSS AUSTRALIA

The amount of land devoted to individual crops varies substantially between states. Use the crop selector to explore which states specialise in each crop and where production is most concentrated.

Wheat has the broadest geographic footprint, with large planted areas across Western Australia, South Australia, New South Wales and Victoria.

Canola is strongest in the southern states, particularly Western Australia, Victoria and New South Wales.

Chickpeas are more concentrated in eastern Australia, while sorghum and cotton are much more strongly centred on Queensland and New South Wales.

THE TAKEAWAY

A large footprint, an uneven landscape

Grazing accounts for nearly 90% of agricultural-use land, but the national total hides strong geographic differences. Queensland and New South Wales have the highest agricultural shares of their landscapes, while Victoria has a more even balance of grazing and cropping.

Land allocation and economic value also follow different paths: cropping expanded between 2010–11 and 2020–21, while the later horticulture series shows growth in fruit and vegetable values alongside a partial recovery in wine grapes. Together, the charts show why land area, location and value need to be read alongside one another.
